CIRCLES · a study in sound · October 2026

Music made
in circles

Twelve instruments for CIRCLES, each from a music people play in a ring, passing parts between them: Balinese gamelan, Shona mbira, Andean panpipes, the Ugandan amadinda, the kora, circle singing, the long loops of Eno and the pulse of In C.

Everything you hear could be played on a CHOMPI: the keys from C3 to C5 with the octave shift, up to fifteen players, loops at CIRCLES' lengths. All of it is rendered through CIRCLES' own room, with each player at a place on its circle. Headphones show the circle best. The firmware is unchanged; this is a sketchbook.

Six knobs, one languageEvery instrument answers to strike, brightness and decay, and with CHOMPI to shimmer, feel and prep. You learn the panel once.
Each has its own preparationThe sixth knob is what that tradition adds to the bare instrument: the damping hand, bottle caps, loose keys, a bolt between the strings.
Built for the platformModal banks, waveguides and formant filters, no samples. Each voice costs about 2 to 6 of today's mallet voices.
Heard in the circleEach piece places its players round the listener, so interlocking parts come from different sides, as they do in the real ensembles.

Eight pieces

Each one is a CIRCLES setup: players with figures, looping at ratios of L0. The ring shows where each player stands around you, in front at the top.

Twelve instruments

Each demo plays the same figure twice: first the bare instrument, then with its preparation (or a second setting, where named). The cost is the CPU per voice against today's mallet voice, measured on a PC.

Today, then new

The same notes on today's CIRCLES preset, then on the instrument that would take its place. Both at the same loudness.

What is not done yet

  • I tuned these by measuring: spectra, levels, decays, no clicks, no DC, peaks under −1 dBFS. I can't listen, so every instrument needs your ears, and some will need a few changes.
  • The CPU figures come from a PC. On the CHOMPI, the load guard would allow fewer notes at once with the heavier voices (siku, bowed glass, tape at 5 to 6 mallets each). Siku, voices and tape have obvious savings left: tables instead of noise generators, shared formant filters.
  • The knob meanings are proposals. Prep in particular changes what K3 with CHOMPI does per instrument; on the CHOMPI it would show on the keys like any other value.
  • The gamelan and amadinda pieces use twelve-tone notes, because the CHOMPI's keys are twelve-tone. The real tunings (pelog, slendro, the Ugandan near-equal pentatonic) would be the first thing to try with the octave or a tuning option.
